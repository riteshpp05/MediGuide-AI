## Case N1 — Acute Ischemic Stroke

**Question:**
A 72-year-old male with atrial fibrillation suddenly cannot move his right arm and leg, has difficulty speaking, and his face is drooping on the right side. Symptoms started 1 hour ago. What is happening and what is the treatment window?

**Expected Behavior:**
- ✅ Emergency banner triggered (category: `stroke`)
- ✅ Acute ischemic stroke recognized (AFib → cardioembolism)
- ✅ FAST criteria mapped: Face (drooping), Arms (weakness), Speech (difficulty), Time (1 hour)
- ✅ Treatment window: IV thrombolysis (tPA) within 4.5 hours, mechanical thrombectomy within 24 hours for large vessel occlusion
- ✅ Immediate action: Call 911, note time of onset, do NOT give aspirin before imaging

## Case N2 — Meningitis

**Question:**
A 20-year-old college student has a severe headache, high fever of 39.5°C, stiff neck, sensitivity to light, and a non-blanching rash on her legs. She has been getting worse over the past 6 hours. What could this be?

**Expected Behavior:**
- ✅ Clinical format with patient summary
- ✅ Most dangerous: Bacterial meningitis (meningococcal — non-blanching rash is a red flag)
- ✅ Classic triad: headache + fever + neck stiffness
- ✅ Non-blanching (petechial/purpuric) rash → meningococcemia — medical emergency
- ✅ Immediate action: Emergency services, empirical IV antibiotics ASAP
- ✅ Differential: Bacterial meningitis > Viral meningitis > Subarachnoid hemorrhage

## Case N3 — Migraine vs Tension Headache

**Question:**
A 35-year-old female has recurrent throbbing headaches on one side of her head, accompanied by nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light and sound. The episodes last 4-12 hours and she sometimes sees flashing lights before they start. How is this different from a tension headache?

**Expected Behavior:**
- ✅ Clinical format
- ✅ Migraine with aura identified (visual aura = flashing lights)
- ✅ Comparison: Migraine (unilateral, throbbing, nausea, photophobia, aura) vs Tension (bilateral, pressing/band-like, no nausea, no aura)
- ✅ No emergency — but red flags to watch for mentioned (thunderclap onset, new after 50, neurological deficits)
- ✅ Missing info: frequency, medication use (risk of medication overuse headache)

## Case N4 — Epilepsy / Seizure Disorder

**Question:**
A 25-year-old male has had two episodes of sudden loss of consciousness with jerking movements of all limbs, tongue biting, and urinary incontinence over the past 3 months. Each episode lasted about 2 minutes. What could be the diagnosis and what tests are needed?

**Expected Behavior:**
- ✅ Clinical format
- ✅ Most likely: Epilepsy (generalized tonic-clonic seizures — ≥2 unprovoked seizures)
- ✅ Features supporting seizure: tongue biting, urinary incontinence, post-ictal confusion
- ✅ Differential: Epilepsy > Syncope (cardiac) > Psychogenic non-epileptic seizures
- ✅ Recommended tests: EEG, brain MRI, basic blood work (glucose, electrolytes)
- ✅ Missing info: family history, drug/alcohol use, sleep deprivation

## Case N5 — Parkinson's Disease

**Question:**
A 62-year-old male has noticed a tremor in his right hand at rest that goes away when he reaches for objects. He also walks with small shuffling steps, feels stiff, and his handwriting has become very small. What condition could this be?

**Expected Behavior:**
- ✅ Clinical format
- ✅ Most likely: Parkinson's Disease
- ✅ Cardinal features identified: resting tremor, bradykinesia (shuffling gait), rigidity (stiffness), micrographia (small handwriting)
- ✅ Asymmetric onset (right hand) is typical
- ✅ Differential: Parkinson's > Essential tremor > Drug-induced parkinsonism > Normal pressure hydrocephalus
- ✅ Recommended: Neurologist referral, DaTscan if uncertain

## Case N6 — Sepsis (General Medicine)

**Question:**
A 58-year-old female with a urinary tract infection has developed a high fever of 39.8°C, heart rate of 120 bpm, rapid breathing, confusion, and low blood pressure of 85/50 mmHg. Her skin feels cold and clammy. What is this condition and how urgent is it?

**Expected Behavior:**
- ✅ Clinical format with patient summary
- ✅ Most dangerous: Septic shock (sepsis with hypotension)
- ✅ Sepsis criteria: infection + fever + tachycardia + tachypnea + altered mental status + hypotension
- ✅ Source identified: urinary tract infection
- ✅ Immediate action: Emergency services, IV fluids, blood cultures, empirical antibiotics
- ✅ Warning: Sepsis mortality increases 7-8% for every hour antibiotics are delayed
